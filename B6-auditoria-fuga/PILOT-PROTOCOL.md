# Learner pilot protocol

Purpose: validate that B6 teaches a transferable decision process rather than only the nine answers.

## Participants

Recruit 5–8 representative learners from the intended course level. Include at least one keyboard-only or assistive-technology participant where possible; formal accessibility review remains separate.

Do not collect real confidential prompts or ask participants to reveal personal AI histories.

## Predefined success signals

Set before running the pilot:

- at least 80% distinguish direct disclosure from cumulative inference in their own words;
- at least 80% avoid the claim that every prompt automatically enters model training;
- at least 80% choose both minification and an approved tool appropriately in a novel case;
- at least 80% name one useful post-send action beyond deletion;
- median guided completion time is approximately 6–8 minutes;
- no participant is blocked by navigation, terminology, or feedback.

These are product targets for the pilot, not claims about the current learner population.

## Session

1. Ask the learner what they think a service can learn from several small prompts.
2. Let them complete the widget without coaching.
3. Observe hesitation, backtracking, abandoned controls, and misunderstood feedback.
4. Ask them to explain:
   - who receives a prompt in the fictional scenario;
   - the difference between retention and training;
   - one active multi-clue inference;
   - why a paid plan is not a sufficient safety test;
   - what ATURA changes before and after sending.
5. Give a new scenario not present in the widget:

   > “You want an AI service to summarize a private playtest spreadsheet containing aliases, ages, email addresses, crash logs, and free-text comments.”

6. Ask for the learner's decision and reasoning without showing the widget.
7. Ask which part felt repetitive, unclear, judgmental, or unnecessarily long.

## Data handling

- Prefer facilitator notes without names.
- Do not record screens or audio without explicit approved consent.
- Do not enter pilot notes into the widget.
- Store any research data according to the institution's approved process.
- Report only aggregate outcomes when participant counts are small enough to risk re-identification.

## Revision gate

Before release, record:

- participant count and course context;
- completion-time range and median;
- each success signal;
- top three misconceptions;
- accessibility or navigation blockers;
- exact content/UI changes made in response.

Do not add population-level claims such as “almost nobody notices” unless the study design and evidence support them.
